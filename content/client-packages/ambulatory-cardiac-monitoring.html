Proactive technical oversight

When a study drifts off track, the support team acts

Two separate teams support a live study. The clinical ECG monitoring team reviews ECG data and communicates qualifying findings according to the ordered service and the physician's prescribed notification protocol. The proactive patient technical-support team is a different function: it follows operational status such as battery, electrode contact, Bluetooth, cellular connection and data transmission.

When the system shows an interruption, the technical-support team contacts the patient when intervention is needed and guides practical next steps, so the study can continue as prescribed.

Issues the team can help address

Battery

Low power or charging interruption.

Electrodes

Adhesion, placement or signal-quality concerns.

Connectivity

Bluetooth or cellular connection interruption.

Transmission

Data gaps or prolonged loss of study communication.

Frequently asked questions

Ambulatory cardiac monitoring, explained

What is ambulatory cardiac monitoring?

It records a patient's ECG while the patient continues normal daily activities outside a clinic or hospital. Depending on the physician's order, the study may use Holter, Extended Holter, Event Monitoring or Mobile Cardiac Telemetry.

How long can an ambulatory cardiac monitoring study last?

Duration depends on the prescribed test. Holter studies commonly run 24–48 hours; Long-Term Holter Monitoring runs more than 48 hours through 7 days, Extended Holter Monitoring more than 7 days through 14 days, and Cardiac Event Monitoring and Mobile Cardiac Telemetry (MCT) 1–30 days.

What is the difference between Holter, Event Monitoring and MCT?

Holter and Extended Holter record ECG data for a defined continuous period and clinical results are generally delivered in the final report. Event Monitoring and MCT can support qualifying finding notifications during the prescribed study according to the physician's notification protocol. MCT adds continuous mobile telemetry oversight.

Does Specialized Medical contact patients when a technical issue occurs?

Yes. When a live-streaming configuration indicates a battery, electrode, connectivity or transmission problem, the support team can contact the patient to troubleshoot and help keep the study on track.

What equipment is used?

The primary featured system is the compact dual-disk S‑Patch. It supports Holter, longer Holter, Event Monitoring and MCT configurations.

Do practices need to purchase monitors?

No equipment purchase is required for the program. Specialized Medical provides monitoring equipment and routine study supplies based on program needs.

How do patients record symptoms?

Patients can use the dedicated phone's simplified Log Symptoms function. Symptom entries can be associated with the study timeline for reporting.

Does the practice receive live test-status visibility?

Live-streaming configurations can provide operational visibility into test status and transmission continuity. A non-live Holter configuration does not provide live test-status visibility.

What does the final report include?

The physician-ready report is designed to organize ECG findings, study statistics and patient symptom context for physician review, interpretation and electronic signature.

Can a practice evaluate the program before committing?

Yes. Practices can request a small no-risk pilot program to evaluate the monitoring workflow with a limited number of patients before deciding whether the service is a fit.
