What is live ECG monitoring?

Live ECG monitoring transmits ambulatory rhythm information during the prescribed study instead of waiting until the wearable is returned.In the Specialized Medical workflow, the wearable monitor communicates with an assigned phone by Bluetooth. The phone sends available ECG information through cellular service into the monitoring workflow when connectivity is available.

Live transmission can support continuous ECG monitoring, remote ECG oversight and earlier visibility into qualifying Event or MCT findings. It does not mean that one person manually watches every beat or that every transmission and communication is instantaneous.

What happens after an interruption?

The system is designed to reconnect when service becomes available after a temporary interruption. Transmission still depends on monitor operation, Bluetooth, phone power and status, network availability and successful data receipt. Uninterrupted coverage and instantaneous transmission are not guaranteed.

Two distinct responsibilities

Operational visibility is not the same as clinical notification

Two separate teams support a live study. Clinical ECG monitoring personnel review ECG data and communicate qualifying findings according to the prescribed service. Proactive patient-support personnel identify technical interruptions, contact patients and help troubleshoot.

Frequently asked questions

Live ECG monitoring answers

What is live ECG monitoring?

Live ECG monitoring is ambulatory monitoring in which rhythm information is transmitted during the prescribed study rather than waiting for the wearable to be returned. In the Specialized Medical workflow, the monitor communicates with an assigned phone, which transmits available ECG information when connectivity is available.

Is live ECG monitoring the same as Mobile Cardiac Telemetry?

Mobile Cardiac Telemetry is a principal live ambulatory ECG service, but live transmission can also support connected Holter, longer Holter and Event Monitoring configurations. Clinical communication timing still depends on the prescribed service.

Is someone manually watching every heartbeat at all times?

No. The service processes and reviews ECG information within the monitoring workflow. Live should not be described as one dedicated person manually watching every heartbeat continuously or as instantaneous communication under every condition.

What operational information can Specialized Medical see during a supported live study?

The team can follow supported indicators such as monitor battery, electrode contact and quality, monitor-to-phone Bluetooth connection, phone-to-network cellular connection and successful ECG data receipt.

What happens when a live study loses cellular coverage?

Transmission may be delayed until connectivity returns. The system is designed to reconnect when service becomes available, but uninterrupted coverage and instantaneous transmission are not guaranteed.

Does the assigned phone need to remain near the patient?

Yes. In the connected workflow, the assigned phone is the gateway between the wearable monitor and the cellular network. It should remain powered, charged and near the patient according to the supplied instructions.

When are Holter findings presented?

For Holter and Long-Term or Extended Holter studies, clinical findings are presented after the final report is generated, even when the connected study provides live operational test-status visibility.

When are Event and MCT findings presented?

Qualifying findings from Cardiac Event Monitoring and Mobile Cardiac Telemetry may be presented during the study according to the ordering physician's prescribed notification protocol, followed by a final report.

Is live ECG monitoring an emergency-response service?

No. It is a diagnostic ambulatory monitoring service and does not replace 911, emergency medical services or the treating physician's emergency instructions. Patients with urgent symptoms should follow their emergency instructions immediately.

Can live ECG monitoring be used after hospital discharge?

Yes. A treating physician may prescribe ambulatory rhythm monitoring after discharge when appropriate, including selected post-TAVR patients under the heart team's patient-selection and notification protocol.
