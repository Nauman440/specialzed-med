What does a turnkey cardiac monitoring program do for a cardiology practice?

It gives the practice a defined operating model for ambulatory monitoring—from the first patient order through the signed final report—without leaving staff to assemble equipment, support, reporting and escalation processes on their own.

The practical value: cardiologists retain control of the prescribed test, notification parameters and final interpretation while Specialized Medical supports implementation, active-study operations, patient troubleshooting and reporting workflow.

Operational visibility

Support the active study without shifting the burden back to your staff

Two separate teams support the active study. The clinical ECG monitoring team reviews ECG data—24/7 for applicable services such as Cardiac Event Monitoring and Mobile Cardiac Telemetry—and communicates qualifying findings according to the physician-defined notification protocol. The proactive patient technical-support team follows operational indicators such as battery status, electrode contact, monitor-to-phone connection, cellular connectivity and successful ECG transmission; when an indicator falls outside the expected range, it contacts the patient and helps troubleshoot.

Dedicated patient phone

A preconfigured phone keeps the patient experience limited to essential monitoring functions. Specialized Medical's Always Connected technology supports five cellular networks across two countries: AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon in the United States, and Telcel and AT&T Mexico in Mexico. This expanded connectivity helps maintain live ECG data transmission in supported monitoring configurations, subject to available coverage and network conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Cardiology-practice program answers

What does turnkey cardiac monitoring mean for a cardiology practice?

It means the program supports the operational workflow around ambulatory monitoring, including implementation, user configuration, staff training, patient enrollment, device hookup, monitoring support, report delivery and physician review—not only the monitoring equipment.

Which cardiac monitoring studies can the program support?

Specialized Medical supports Holter, Long-Term and Extended Holter, Cardiac Event Monitoring and Mobile Cardiac Telemetry workflows. The ordering physician selects the appropriate test and duration for each patient.

Can patients be pre-enrolled before their appointments?

Yes. Practices can prepare a patient record before the hookup appointment so staff can confirm the order, duration and notification instructions more efficiently when the patient arrives.

Is cardiology-practice staff training included?

Yes. Implementation can include role-based training for front-office staff, medical assistants, nurses, physicians and administrators, along with practice-specific workflow and notification-protocol review.

What happens after the patient leaves the office?

For supported live workflows, Specialized Medical follows relevant operational test-status indicators and assists the patient when battery, electrode, phone, cellular or transmission issues occur. Clinical communication follows the ordered service and physician-prescribed protocol.

Can the program support multiple cardiology locations?

Yes. User permissions, inventory, patient enrollment, support, report routing and training can be organized for centralized or location-specific operations across multiple offices.

Can physicians review and sign reports electronically?

Yes. When configured, the portal supports physician review, interpretation, dating and electronic signature of final reports while the physician remains responsible for the final interpretation.

Does Specialized Medical integrate with every EMR?

Integration and report-delivery options depend on the practice's EMR, technical capabilities and implementation configuration. Available options should be confirmed during workflow discovery rather than assumed.

Does Specialized Medical guarantee reimbursement?

No. Specialized Medical can provide billing-support information and workflow templates, but the practice remains responsible for coding, documentation, medical necessity, payer requirements, compliance and claim submission. Payment is not guaranteed.

How can a cardiology practice evaluate the program?

A practice can request a workflow demonstration or discuss a small no-obligation pilot. The team reviews the current process, confirms service fit, configures the pilot and evaluates the results before broader rollout.
